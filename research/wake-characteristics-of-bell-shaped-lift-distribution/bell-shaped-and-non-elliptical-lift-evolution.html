Research Subcategory

Bell-Shaped and Non-Elliptical Lift Evolution

Wake Characteristics of Bell-Shaped Lift Distribution / Bell-Shaped and Non-Elliptical Lift Evolution

This research explores how the wake changes when spanwise lift distribution moves away from the classical elliptical form toward bell-shaped and other non-elliptical loadings. The studies compare multiple wing configurations and show that changing the way circulation tapers near the tip can reshape the wake in fundamental ways, including weaker downwash concentration, delayed roll-up, and vorticity structures that remain flatter and more inboard than those associated with a conventional tip vortex. The emphasis is on the wake as a dynamic expression of loading choice, not merely as a downstream byproduct.

Why It Matters

Much of classical wing design treats elliptical loading as the natural reference point, especially when minimizing induced drag for a fixed span. These studies broaden that conversation by showing that under different structural or geometric constraints, other lift distributions can produce wake behavior that may be aerodynamically advantageous in different ways. That matters because it reframes wake control as a spanloading problem at the design stage. Instead of asking only how to weaken a vortex after it forms, the work asks how a wing can be loaded so that the familiar vortex-centered wake is altered from the beginning.

Research Focus

The work combines force measurements, streamwise and cross-stream PIV, simplified wake modeling, and supporting CFD to compare wake development across elliptic and non-elliptic loading cases. Particular attention is given to where circulation concentrates, how quickly the wake rolls up near the tip, and how these behaviors change between design and off-design conditions. By isolating bell-shaped loading on simpler twisted wings as well as studying broader non-elliptical families, the research builds a more general understanding of how lift distribution governs wake evolution and offers a physically grounded route toward lower-vorticity, more efficient lifting-surface design.
