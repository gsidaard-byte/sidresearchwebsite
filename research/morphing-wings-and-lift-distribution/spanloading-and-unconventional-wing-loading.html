Spanloading and Unconventional Wing Loading

Morphing Wings and Lift Distribution / Spanloading and Unconventional Wing Loading

This work explores spanwise loading as a primary aerodynamic design variable, using an oblique flying wing to study how twist distribution and slew angle reshape performance and stability. Instead of starting from the assumption that a wing’s planform alone determines its behavior, the research examines how deliberately redistributing lift along the span can change rolling, pitching, and yawing tendencies as well as the character of the wake. The result is a more design-centered view of spanloading, where aerodynamic efficiency and stability are treated as consequences of how lift is arranged across the wing rather than how much lift is produced overall.

Why It Matters

Unconventional aircraft concepts often promise efficiency gains but run into stability and control challenges once the full aerodynamic picture is considered. That is especially true for tailless and oblique-wing configurations, where the usual control architecture is no longer available to clean up unfavorable moments. By showing how twist-driven load redistribution can moderate moment variation and influence directional behavior, this research highlights spanloading as a practical lever for making unusual aircraft concepts more manageable across the flight envelope. It matters because it connects high-level aerodynamic theory to the real design problem of making unconventional wings fly well, not just look interesting on paper.

Research Focus

The study uses parametric variations in geometric twist together with computational aerodynamic analysis to examine how different load distributions affect the aerodynamic center, force coefficients, and stability-related moments at multiple slew angles. Particular interest is given to bell-shaped loading ideas inspired by Prandtl and Horten, including the possibility of influencing yaw behavior through inboard-shifted lift and proverse-yaw tendencies. Even where the target loading is not fully achieved, the work makes clear that spanwise twist is powerful enough to substantially reshape moment behavior, offering a path toward stability improvement through aerodynamic load design rather than after-the-fact correction.
