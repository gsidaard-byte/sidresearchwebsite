Research Subcategory

Non-Contact Optical Diagnostics

Flow Diagnostics and Event-Based Sensing / Non-Contact Optical Diagnostics

This work advances optical measurement techniques for situations where conventional probes are too intrusive and standard imaging methods do not provide the needed access. The research includes Doppler lidar for velocity measurement in wind-tunnel flows and back-imaged pressure-sensitive paint for resolving the surface-pressure footprint of propellers operating near the ground. Both approaches are built around the same experimental priority: obtaining useful flow information while disturbing the flow as little as possible. The result is a diagnostic capability that reaches difficult geometries and boundary conditions without sacrificing physical relevance.

Why It Matters

Many of the most important aerodynamic questions arise precisely where measurement is hardest: near surfaces, inside confined geometries, or in regions where the sensing method itself can alter the physics being studied. Non-contact optical diagnostics address that problem directly. Doppler lidar offers a path toward remote velocity measurement in wind tunnels, while back-imaged pressure-sensitive paint opens a global view of unsteady pressure behavior over surfaces influenced by rotor wakes and ground effect. That matters because performance, control response, and safety often depend on spatial patterns and unsteady signatures that cannot be inferred confidently from a small number of point sensors alone.

Research Focus

A key thrust is method development under realistic laboratory constraints. The Doppler lidar study builds a low-cost single-component system, develops a predictive framework for power and detector sensitivity, and tests the approach in a small-scale wind tunnel where the instrument captures acceleration through the contraction into the test section. The pressure-sensitive paint study extends optical diagnostics into a challenging rotor-ground configuration by using back-imaging to recover both mean and unsteady surface-pressure fields. Frequency-domain analysis then connects the measured pressure fluctuations to blade-passing dynamics and to the spatial organization of the under-rotor flow, including regions associated with changes in amplitude distribution and dividing-streamline behavior. Together, the work shows how carefully designed optical methods can reveal both velocity and pressure information in places where direct access is limited.
