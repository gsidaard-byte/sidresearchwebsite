Embedded Wake Sensing and Flow-Response Testbeds

Flow Diagnostics and Event-Based Sensing / Embedded Wake Sensing and Flow-Response Testbeds

Research in this area explores sensing strategies in which the measurement device is itself a flexible flow-responsive structure. The core studies use piezo-embedded inverted PVDF flags placed in the wake of airfoils to quantify how voltage response, oscillation frequency, and structural motion vary with angle of attack, downstream position, and wake unsteadiness. Instead of treating sensing as a purely external imaging task, the work asks how a lightweight embedded element can become part of the aerodynamic system and still return physically meaningful information about the surrounding flow. The result is a compact approach to wake detection that connects materials, fluid response, and instrumentation.

Why It Matters

Small air vehicles and distributed sensing platforms often face strict constraints on size, mass, power, and instrumentation complexity. In those settings, a sensing concept that is lightweight, locally responsive, and potentially multifunctional is especially valuable. A piezoelectric structure that can both respond to wake dynamics and harvest energy points toward a class of flow-aware systems that do more than passively observe. It offers a possible route to embedded monitoring, wake detection, and future control architectures in which sensing is distributed directly into the vehicle or test article rather than added as a separate diagnostic layer.

Research Focus

The main effort centers on understanding how inverted piezoelectric flags behave in unsteady wakes and whether their response can be trusted as a wake signature rather than merely a structural artifact. The studies quantify amplitude and frequency response behind NACA 0012 and SD7003 wings, compare behavior in freestream and wake conditions, and examine how increasing angle of attack changes the strength and character of the signal. Particle image velocimetry is used to verify that the sensor does not significantly corrupt the wake it is intended to measure, while RMS voltage contours reveal recognizable features such as wake stratification and downwash patterns. The broader technical aim is to establish flexible embedded materials as credible aerodynamic sensors and as candidate components in future closed-loop or endurance-oriented flight systems.
