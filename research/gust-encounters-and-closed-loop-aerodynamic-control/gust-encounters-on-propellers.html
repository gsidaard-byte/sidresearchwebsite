Gust Encounters on Propellers

Gust Encounters and Closed-Loop Aerodynamic Control / Gust Encounters on Propellers

Research in this area examines how propellers respond when the incoming flow is no longer steady, especially in the forward-flight and edgewise-flight conditions that are increasingly relevant to UAVs, air taxis, and other distributed-propulsion systems. The work studies sinusoidal streamwise gusts, changes in rotor incidence angle, and tandem propeller arrangements to understand how thrust, power, and pitching response evolve when a propeller must operate inside a time-varying aerodynamic environment rather than a fixed one. A central result is that propeller gust response is shaped not only by the disturbance itself, but also by rotor orientation and by wake interaction between neighboring propellers.

Why It Matters

Unsteady inflow is a practical flight condition, not a special case. Small aircraft and multi-rotor vehicles routinely encounter atmospheric disturbances, aggressive maneuvers, building wakes, and transition regimes where the relative flow seen by a rotor changes rapidly. This work matters because it helps define when a propeller behaves in a largely quasi-steady way and when that approximation begins to break down, with measurable reductions in mean thrust and clear phase lag in the response at higher reduced frequencies. That distinction is important for performance prediction, control design, and propulsion-system reliability in disturbed air.

Research Focus

The studies combine wind-tunnel gust generation with direct measurement of thrust, power, and moments on fixed-pitch RC propellers under varying incidence and tandem arrangements. One thread shows that at lower reduced frequencies, propeller response can often be predicted from steady-performance data using linear superposition, while higher-frequency forcing reveals more clearly unsteady behavior. Another thread extends the same question to tandem configurations, where the rear propeller becomes especially sensitive to upstream wake effects. Taken together, the work builds a propulsion-specific foundation for understanding gust encounters, rather than treating propellers as a minor extension of wing gust physics.
