Propeller-Wake Spray Transport and Distortion

Understanding Spray Drift / Propeller-Wake Spray Transport and Distortion

This research studies what happens when an agricultural spray is released into the highly unsteady, three-dimensional wake of a propeller. Rather than treating the nozzle as an isolated spray source, the work examines how rotor-induced flow reshapes the plume after atomization, altering its width, depth, concentration field, and downstream coherence. Across flat-fan nozzles and rotary atomizers, the studies show that propeller wakes do not merely advect droplets; they actively reorganize the spray, sometimes widening the plume, flattening or contracting its natural shape, and redistributing where deposition-relevant droplets remain concentrated.

Why It Matters

For aerial application, the propeller wake is part of the delivery system. If that wake is ignored, both drift predictions and measurement results can become misleading. The research shows that finer sprays are especially vulnerable to wake-driven distortion, while conventional measurement tools such as laser diffraction can become biased when the plume widens, oscillates, or sheds droplets away from the sampling region. That matters for both regulation and practice: accurate drift assessment depends on understanding not only droplet formation, but also how vehicle-induced airflow changes what reaches the crop and what escapes it.

Research Focus

The work combines multiple experimental approaches to resolve wake-driven spray transport from several angles. Laser diffraction, shadowgraphy, gutter testing, and optical spray patternation are used to quantify droplet distributions and three-dimensional plume geometry beneath single- and multi-propeller configurations. The studies compare standard agricultural nozzles and rotary atomizers, showing, for example, that finer hydraulic nozzles can experience dramatic plume widening and depth loss, while rotary atomizers can transition from a natural umbrella-shaped plume to a contracted or more cylindrical spray depending on the wake structure. The overall focus is on connecting spray geometry, droplet transport, and measurement fidelity in the actual aerodynamic environment of UAV spraying.
