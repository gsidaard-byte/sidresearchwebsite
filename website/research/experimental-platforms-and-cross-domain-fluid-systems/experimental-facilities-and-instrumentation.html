Research Subcategory

Experimental Facilities and Instrumentation

Experimental Platforms and Cross-Domain Fluid Systems / Experimental Facilities and Instrumentation

This research treats the experimental platform itself as a scientific contribution. Rather than viewing tunnels, flow generators, and test hardware as background infrastructure, the work studies how facility design, actuation strategy, optical access, manufacturability, and dynamic response shape the quality of the aerodynamic knowledge that follows. The projects include a shuttered open-jet wind tunnel for unsteady-flow generation and an additive-manufactured supersonic wind tunnel designed for both education and research. Together, they show that meaningful advances in fluid mechanics often begin with building better environments in which to ask the question.

Why It Matters

A research result is only as trustworthy as the platform that produces it. If an unsteady wind tunnel cannot generate controlled disturbances with known frequency content, or if a low-cost supersonic facility cannot deliver a credible flow path and usable optical access, then later measurements and interpretations rest on weak foundations. This work matters because it strengthens that foundation. It expands access to specialized testing capability, improves experimental reproducibility, and creates facilities that support both fundamental studies and hands-on learning. In practical terms, it helps make sophisticated aerodynamic experimentation more flexible, more affordable, and more defensible.

Research Focus

One part of the work characterizes a shuttered Eiffel-type open-jet tunnel using hot-wire anemometry and flow visualization to understand how louver actuation modifies the test-section response, shear-layer structure, and vortex dynamics. That effort is directly relevant to gust generation and controlled unsteady testing. The second part develops a Mach-2-plus blowdown tunnel fabricated through additive manufacturing in high-performance thermoplastic, with modular converging-diverging nozzles, optical access, and CFD-backed design validation. Experimental schlieren testing is then used to check operating behavior and confirm that the facility can support future research and instruction. Across both efforts, the priority is not only to build hardware, but to quantify how that hardware behaves so later aerodynamic conclusions can be trusted.
