Research Subcategory

Wing-Embedded Propulsion

Propeller Aerodynamics and Distributed Propulsion / Wing-Embedded Propulsion

This research explores propulsion systems in which the propeller and wing must be understood as one aerodynamic unit. Embedding or partially embedding a propeller within a wing changes lift, drag, forward thrust, wake deflection, and control response in ways that cannot be inferred reliably from isolated wing and isolated propeller data alone. The work spans thrust-vectoring concepts with downstream vanes, inlet and opening geometry effects, propeller incidence effects in forward flight, and broader questions about when simple performance superposition is valid.

Why It Matters

Wing-embedded propulsion is attractive because it promises compact integration, multifunctional lifting surfaces, and new control possibilities for VTOL and distributed-electric aircraft. But those advantages depend on how the installation actually reshapes the flow. The studies show that circular openings can outperform other opening shapes, that forward propeller tilt can widen the useful thrust-vectoring envelope, and that placement along the span changes how much the wing is penalized or helped by the installation. They also show that lift may sometimes be estimated reasonably well by adding standalone wing and propeller effects, while forward thrust and drag often remain much harder to predict because the coupling is stronger and more nonlinear.

Research Focus

The work combines wind-tunnel testing, force and moment measurements, geometry variation, and numerical analysis to understand installation-sensitive behavior in practical configurations. Some studies focus on control-oriented performance, such as how vane deflection, propeller tilt, and inlet shaping affect vectoring authority and pitching moment generation. Others focus on system-level aerodynamics, including the influence of hole shape, spanwise opening location, and propeller diameter on lift and forward thrust. Across the research, the central goal is to determine when wing-embedded propulsion can be treated with reduced-order design logic and when the installed configuration must be analyzed as a tightly coupled flow problem.
