Research Subcategory

Tandem Propeller

Propeller Aerodynamics and Distributed Propulsion / Tandem Propeller

This research investigates what happens when two propellers operate in tandem with partial overlap, vertical offset, and varying streamwise spacing. Rather than behaving like two isolated rotors placed side by side in a layout drawing, the pair functions as a coupled aerodynamic system in which the front propeller alters the inflow, wake angle, and loading seen by the rear propeller. The studies trace how that interaction changes across edgewise and forward-flight conditions, and how stagger direction, spacing, and overlap reshape the performance of the full system.

Why It Matters

Compact multirotor and distributed-propulsion vehicles are always balancing footprint, efficiency, and controllability. Tandem layouts can save space, but they also invite strong wake interference. The results show that geometry matters sharply: negative stagger can produce meaningful efficiency improvements, while positive stagger can impose larger penalties when the front wake strikes the rear propeller more directly. The work also identifies practical design thresholds, including the finding that increasing vertical separation helps substantially up to about half a diameter, after which the improvement begins to saturate. That kind of result is directly useful when deciding how much packaging complexity is worth carrying into a real aircraft.

Research Focus

The studies combine separate thrust and torque measurements on front and rear propellers with smoke visualization, PIV, and inflow-angle analysis to understand not just whether performance changes, but why. A recurring focus is the rear propeller, since its performance is especially sensitive to the distorted inflow created upstream. The later work uses measured wake structure from a standalone propeller to build a linear-superposition-based prediction method for tandem arrangements, showing that even if exact inflow values are difficult to match, the major performance trends can still be estimated with useful accuracy. The result is a progression from empirical characterization toward design-oriented prediction of tandem rotor placement.
