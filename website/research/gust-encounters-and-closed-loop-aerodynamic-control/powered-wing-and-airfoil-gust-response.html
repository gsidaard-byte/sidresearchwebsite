Powered-Wing and Airfoil Gust Response

Gust Encounters and Closed-Loop Aerodynamic Control / Powered-Wing and Airfoil Gust Response

Research in this area investigates how a wing responds to gusts when propulsion is integrated directly into the lifting system. The work studies powered wings subjected to periodic and streamwise gusts over a range of reduced frequencies, propeller locations, and angles of attack, with attention to how lift and pitching moment differ from the unpowered case. A key theme is that propulsion does more than add thrust: it changes the local flow around the wing, alters separation behavior, and reshapes the way the aerodynamic system experiences unsteady inflow.

Why It Matters

Many emerging aircraft concepts rely on tightly coupled wing-propeller systems, especially in low-altitude operations where gusts, wake interactions, and transition maneuvers are unavoidable. This research matters because it helps define the uncontrolled aerodynamic baseline for such systems before active mitigation is layered on top. The results show that powered and unpowered wings can behave similarly at low angle of attack, yet diverge meaningfully as frequency and angle of attack increase, particularly when added propeller energy helps delay or suppress separation. That makes gust response a system-integration problem rather than a wing-only problem.

Research Focus

The studies use full-span powered wing models in an unsteady wind-tunnel environment where streamwise gusts are generated in a controlled way and the resulting lift and pitching-moment histories are measured. Particular attention is given to reduced-frequency effects, propeller spanwise placement, and the phase relationship between gust forcing and aerodynamic response. One thread examines how powered-wing behavior compares with theoretical predictions in cruise-like conditions, while another maps how the differences between powered and unpowered wings grow at higher angles of attack and stronger unsteadiness. The broader goal is to understand how propulsion-airframe coupling influences gust sensitivity before control strategies are introduced.
