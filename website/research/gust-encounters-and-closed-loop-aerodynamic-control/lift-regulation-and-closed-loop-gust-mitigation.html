Research Subcategory

Lift Regulation and Closed-Loop Gust Mitigation

Gust Encounters and Closed-Loop Aerodynamic Control / Lift Regulation and Closed-Loop Gust Mitigation

This research explores how aerodynamic loading can be actively regulated in real time when a wing is operating in strongly unsteady conditions. The work begins with closed-loop lift tracking on pitching lifting surfaces and extends into the direct mitigation of periodic and discrete vortical gust encounters using feedback control. Rather than assuming that the flow must first be modeled perfectly before control becomes possible, the studies show that force sensing, actuation, and carefully tuned controllers can be used to command meaningful lift behavior even when the flow is separated, rapidly changing, or dominated by evolving vortical structures.

Why It Matters

Disturbance rejection in aerodynamics is often limited by the gap between idealized models and the actual behavior of separated or vortical flow. This line of work matters because it demonstrates that closed-loop control can remain effective even when the flow physics are too complicated for simple low-order assumptions to capture on their own. That has direct relevance to gust alleviation, post-stall maneuvering, resilient low-speed flight, and the design of vehicles that must remain controllable in environments where the incoming flow is dynamic rather than well behaved. It also clarifies an important experimental truth: meaningful mitigation results depend on both a capable controller and a well-characterized disturbance.

Research Focus

The studies develop and compare PID and state-feedback control architectures for lift tracking, then connect those control outcomes to the underlying flow using force measurements, PIV, and time-resolved PIV. The work shows that harmonic lift targets can be achieved even beyond the attached-flow regime, though the required angle-of-attack histories become strongly non-harmonic as the controller compensates for changing vortex dynamics. In the gust-mitigation studies, periodic and discrete vortical disturbances are generated upstream and the downstream lift response is measured with and without control, revealing substantial reductions in entry transients and more limited improvement during recovery. Across the body of work, the emphasis is on closing the loop between disturbance structure, aerodynamic response, and actuation strategy.
