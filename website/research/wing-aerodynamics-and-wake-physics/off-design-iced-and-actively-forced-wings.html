Research Subcategory

Off-Design, Iced, and Actively Forced Wings

Wing Aerodynamics and Wake Physics / Off-Design, Iced, and Actively Forced Wings

This research examines wing behavior in conditions where baseline aerodynamic assumptions no longer hold. The work spans ice-accreted airfoils, actively blown wings, and dynamically pitching delta wings with leading-edge-vortex control, all of which push the flow into regimes shaped by separation, contamination, actuation, or rapid motion. Rather than treating these cases as minor perturbations of a clean wing, the studies show that off-design conditions reorganize the flow in fundamental ways, changing coherent structures, wake development, vortex stability, and the relationship between geometry and performance.

Why It Matters

Aircraft do not operate only in ideal conditions. Ice, unsteady maneuvers, and active flow control all introduce aerodynamic states that are difficult to predict from steady clean-wing behavior alone. This research matters because it addresses the reliability and controllability of aerodynamic systems in those harder regimes. The icing studies speak directly to safety, showing that scaled or numerically predicted ice shapes can fail to reproduce the actual aerodynamic penalties seen in supercooled large-drop conditions. The blown-wing and delta-wing studies matter for emerging vehicle concepts because they clarify both the promise and the limits of actuation when flow structures become strongly nonlinear or unsteady.

Research Focus

The work focuses on identifying the flow mechanisms that govern off-design performance and on testing whether common predictive approaches remain trustworthy. In the icing studies, particle image velocimetry and modal analysis are used to compare full-scale, scaled, and LEWICE-generated ice shapes, revealing that geometric similarity alone does not guarantee aerodynamic similarity. In the active-flow-control studies, a blown jet is used to influence leading-edge-vortex behavior on a pitching delta wing, with results showing measurable benefits at higher angle of attack but reduced effectiveness as unsteadiness increases. Complementary blown-wing modeling uses machine learning to map how parameters such as thrust coefficient, flap angle, geometry, and engine placement influence lift and pitching moment, providing a broader system-level view of how actuation changes aerodynamic response.
