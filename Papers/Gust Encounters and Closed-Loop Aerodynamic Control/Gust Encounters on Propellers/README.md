# Gust Encounters on Propellers

Parent category: **Gust Encounters and Closed-Loop Aerodynamic Control**

## Important Findings

This subcategory isolates the propeller side of the gust problem. The papers show that unsteady inflow changes rotary-system loading in ways that depend on geometry, operating incidence, and whether the propulsion system is working alone or in tandem. That makes propeller gust response a distinct aerodynamic regime rather than a simple extension of wing gust response.

## Value

Its value is that it provides a propulsion-specific baseline for unsteady flow response. That baseline is useful for distributed propulsion, small UAV design, and any study where propulsion and disturbance rejection need to be considered together instead of separately.

## Key Takeaways

Propeller response to gusts depends on incidence, tandem arrangement, and disturbance periodicity, so propulsion studies need their own unsteady-flow baselines.

## Papers

- **Sinusoidal Gust Response of RC Propellers in Tandem Configuration** (C3_SinusoidalGustResponseofRCPropellersinTandemConfigurationFinalV3.pdf)
  Establishes how tandem propellers respond to periodic gust forcing and how mutual interference alters the load response.
- **Sinusoidal Gust Response of RC Propellers at Different Incidence Angles** (C3_cai-et-al-2022-sinusoidal-gust-response-of-rc-propellers-at-different-incidence-angles.pdf)
  Shows how inflow angle changes the gust sensitivity of propellers and therefore the design envelope for rotating propulsion in unsteady air.
- **Wind (Aerospace Science and Technology, 2023)** (C3_wind-03-00015-v2.pdf)
  Provides the third propeller-focused study in the folder and extends the unsteady-propulsion baseline used by the other two papers.
