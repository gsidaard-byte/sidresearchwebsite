Teaching Philosophy

A student-centered approach to learning.

This teaching philosophy centers curiosity, agency, hands-on exploration, and meaningful connections between engineering ideas and the people they serve.

Section 01 · Foundation

Three principles that ground the work.

Every course decision, from the first day to the last portfolio, traces back to these three commitments.

01

Experiential learning empowers students to use their unique talents.

02

Learning should move beyond grades toward curiosity, reflection, and ownership.

03

Students should leave able to explain ideas clearly, not just repeat equations.

Section 02 · How Learning Happens

Five pillars that build the room.

Pedagogy isn't a single method. It's a small set of practices that reinforce each other across the semester.

Portfolio-based assessment

Students write in their own words, reflect deeply, and build a lasting record of learning.

Story-driven teaching

Concepts organized into a cohesive narrative so each idea builds on the previous one.

Active & interactive learning

Group work, guided notes, discussion, role play, and thought exercises keep students engaged.

Real-world context

Courses connect theory to real aircraft, engineering practice, and authentic design problems.

Inquiry-based learning

Students search, question, connect concepts, and support their narratives with evidence.

Section 03 · Entrepreneurially Minded Learning

The KEEN 3 C's.

Curiosity, Connections, and Creating Value: the entrepreneurial mindset isn't a separate course, it's the connective tissue.

Curiosity

A zest for questions and the pursuit of what is required to answer them well.

Connections

Link equations, concepts, experiences, and personal understanding into a coherent whole.

Creating Value

Use knowledge in meaningful ways: for the work that matters now, future work, and ongoing learning.
